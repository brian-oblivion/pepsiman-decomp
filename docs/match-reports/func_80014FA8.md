## Residue

Everything from the analog block on matches, and so does port 0's half.
Port 1's buttons: retail computes `or; xori 0xFFFF; andi v0, v1, 0xFFFF`
and ands the `andi` result with the old-state mask. The build drops the
`andi` (cc1 knows the `or` of two shifted bytes fits 16 bits), which also
moves the `D_80095870[0]` store up two slots. 117 vs 118 words.

## What was tried (about 30 builds and one permuter run)

- `u16 btn` for both ports: the `andi` survives, but `^ 0xFFFF` on a
  narrowed value folds to `nor` (both ports). Every way of keeping the xor
  in `s32` then truncating (`(u16)` casts at the stores, `raw ^= 0xFFFF;
  btn = raw;`, a separate `s32 raw`, hi/lo byte locals shared by the two
  ports) gives `xori` and loses port 1's `andi`.
- Static inline helpers: `u16 f(u8 *p)` returning the buttons (nor),
  `s32 f(u8 *p, s32 i)`, a two-port `padLatch(u16 b0, u16 b1)` and a
  per-port `padLatch(s32 i, u16 b)` with the stores inside. None keeps the
  `andi`; the two-port one also scrambles the load order. (The
  narrow-inline-parameter lever that matched func_80014DB0 in this round
  does not apply here.)
- Separate scalars for `D_80095854`/`D_8009584C`/`D_80095874` against
  `[1]` of the paired arrays: no byte difference.
- Loading both old states before the stores (`m0`, `m1`) is what puts the
  `D_80095854` load early as retail has it; `m = (u16)btn & m1` before the
  port-1 stores came from the permuter.
