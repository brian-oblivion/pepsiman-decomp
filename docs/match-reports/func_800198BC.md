## How it went

1. A first body with `x`/`y` products in the `if`/`else` and the four
   corners derived from `x0`, `y0`, `x1`, `y1`: 22/263. Retail does four
   extra multiplies: corner 1 recomputes the `y0` products from a second
   copy of `y0` (`$fp`), corner 2 the `x0` products from a spilled copy
   (sp+0x18), while corner 3 reuses corner 1's x and corner 2's y products.
   Its `mult` sits in each arm and the `mflo` after the join.
2. Chained corners at the join (`x0 = x2 = x >> 12;` A3D4-style) changed
   nothing: CSE unifies the copies. A volatile copy made it worse.
3. Both chains INSIDE each arm, `x0 = x2 = (...) >> 12; y0 = y1 = (...) >>
   12;`: at the join CSE no longer knows `x0 == x2` or `y0 == y1`, so the
   products are recomputed; after reload the arms' identical tails (the y
   multiply, `mflo`, shifts, copies) are cross-jumped into the join, which is
   retail's layout. 252/263, the rest only spill-slot order.
4. Declaring the corners before `w`/`h`: reload hands out spill slots in
   pseudo order, so `x2` takes sp+0x18 and `w`/`h` sp+0x20/0x28. 263/263.

### Proposed learning

- Products recomputed from copies of one value that CSE would have merged,
  with a `mult` in each arm of an `if` and the `mflo` after the join: the
  copies were assigned in BOTH arms (a chained assignment per arm). CSE
  loses the equality at the join; cross-jumping merges the identical arm
  tails afterwards.
- Spill slots (`sb`/`sh` to the stack with `$t7` reloads) are laid out in
  declaration order of the spilled locals; reorder declarations to move
  them.
